The main pattern is a two-model regime.
S\_Mamba is the strongest RMSE baseline for 8 horizons, covering every horizon from 1 to 90 days, while iTransformer is strongest for the remaining 4 horizons from 120 to 360 days.
The handover is narrow rather than abrupt: at 120 days the RMSE gap is about 2.05\% in favor of iTransformer, while at 360 days the gap is only about 0.06\%.
This means the long-horizon comparison between the two models is highly competitive rather than dominated by one side.

Autoformer is a stable third-place baseline across all 12 horizons.
However, its overall average RMSE of 38.71 is far above the two leading models, whose overall average RMSE values are 16.04 for S\_Mamba and 16.15 for iTransformer.
The plain attention baselines are substantially weaker on GLD under the locked shared setting, with overall average RMSE above 69 for Transformer and above 76 for Informer and Reformer.

The MAE pattern is similar.
S\_Mamba and iTransformer remain nearly tied overall, and S\_Mamba holds a slightly lower average MAE, 11.61 versus 11.65.
These baseline results identify S\_Mamba and iTransformer as the two reference models that NS-Mamba must improve upon.
