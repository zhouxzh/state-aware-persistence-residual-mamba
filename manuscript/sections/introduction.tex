\section{Introduction}

Time series forecasting models are often evaluated under the assumption that past and future windows are drawn from similar distributions.
This assumption is weak for nonstationary real-world data.
Gold-related exchange-traded products such as GLD can experience abrupt level shifts, volatility changes, macroeconomic regime changes, and long periods of trend persistence or reversal.
These effects are not just noise; they change the forecasting problem itself.

Recent deep forecasting models use attention, decomposition, and state-space mechanisms to model long temporal context.
Mamba is especially attractive because it provides linear-time sequence modeling through selective state spaces \cite{mamba}.
In a forecasting setting, however, using Mamba alone does not guarantee robustness to nonstationarity.
The model may still entangle level, scale, trend, and residual dynamics in one hidden representation.
This motivates an architecture that keeps the efficient Mamba backbone while explicitly exposing nonstationary structure to the network.

This work begins with a strict experimental principle.
Before introducing NS-Mamba, all suitable baselines are evaluated under a shared configuration selected by S\_Mamba calibration.
This prevents model-specific tuning from becoming a hidden source of performance differences.
The same horizon suite and output scale are then used for clean baselines, Mamba variants, and the proposed NS-Mamba models.

The intended contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a controlled GLD forecasting protocol covering horizons from 1 to 360 days;
    \item a clean comparison between Transformer-style, decomposition-based, inverted, and Mamba-based forecasting models;
    \item a nonstationarity-aware Mamba architecture family combining adaptive normalization, decomposition, and shift-aware fusion;
    \item a diagnostic ablation showing that decomposition is the strongest first modification on GLD long-horizon forecasting.
\end{itemize}
