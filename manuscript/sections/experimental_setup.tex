\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Financial Panels and Data Provenance}

The frozen data cutoff is 2026-06-30.
Table~\ref{tab:financial-panels} describes four daily panels after chronological sorting and complete-case intersection.
The foreign-exchange panel uses six FRED exchange-rate series, with reciprocal transformations where needed so that every column is quoted as U.S. dollars per unit of foreign currency \cite{fred}.
The yield panel contains 2-, 5-, 10-, and 30-year U.S. Treasury constant-maturity rates from FRED, with DGS10 as target \cite{fred}.
The cross-asset panel aligns FRED S\&P 500, WTI crude-oil, and VIX levels with the unadjusted GLD close obtained from Yahoo Finance \cite{fred,yahoo-gld}.
The cryptoasset panel uses daily UTC spot-closing prices from the official Binance public archive \cite{binance-data}.
For the latter two panels, redistribution rights must be confirmed before a public data release; the local source archives, URLs, retrieval dates, byte counts, and SHA-256 hashes are retained.

All processed panels have unique increasing dates and no missing cells.
Complete-case alignment avoids imputed targets but produces an irregular observation calendar governed by common market availability; horizons are therefore measured in panel observations, not elapsed calendar days.
The fixed splits are training through 2020-12-31, validation from 2021-01-01 through 2022-12-31, and final evaluation from 2023-01-01 through 2026-06-30.
Validation and test loaders can use preceding observations as input context, but every forecast target remains inside its named split.
Each scaler is fitted only on the training rows.

The legacy GLD and Exchange benchmarks were used during model development and are excluded from the present evidence boundary.
Non-financial application benchmarks are also outside the protocol because the research question is specifically nonstationary financial forecasting rather than broad application-domain ranking.
These exclusions were frozen with the new protocol and are not based on outcomes from its protected final period.

\subsection{Selection-Safe Nonstationarity Characterization}

Before test access, diagnostics read only training and validation targets.
For training levels, the augmented Dickey--Fuller (ADF) test includes a constant and trend, and the KPSS test uses trend stationarity as its null \cite{dickey-fuller,kpss}.
The same tests are applied to first differences with a constant but no trend.
The diagnostic pair is interpreted descriptively: failure to reject an ADF unit-root null together with rejection of the KPSS trend-stationarity null supports treating the observed level as nonstationary, but neither test identifies a unique data-generating mechanism.

Table~\ref{tab:selection-safe-diagnostics} reports the prespecified level tests and validation shifts.
All four training targets show ADF $p>0.36$ and KPSS $p\leq0.01$ for levels.
The validation period also changes materially in at least one location, scale, difference-volatility, or cross-series-correlation summary for every panel.
Cryptoassets show the largest location and difference-volatility shifts, while cross-asset and foreign-exchange panels show the largest maximum absolute changes in feature--target first-difference correlations.

\subsection{Selection and Locked Evaluation}

The learned set contains DLinear, NLinear, PatchTST, TimesNet, iTransformer, S-Mamba, NS-Mamba-D, MS-Mamba-MD, and PMS-Mamba-PD.
Every model--task study receives 12 effective Optuna trials, where completed and pruned trials count toward the fixed budget.
A seeded TPE sampler evaluates calibration horizons 5, 60, and 252; median pruning begins after four startup trials.
The three best completed calibration trials are then replayed across all six validation horizons.
The configuration with the smallest balanced validation RMSSE is frozen for that model and task.
Training uses Adam, at most 10 epochs, early-stopping patience 3, and mean squared error.
Model-specific search spaces are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:frozen-protocol}.

No final-period evaluation is permitted during selection.
The selection audit verifies task and model coverage, protocol and data hashes, horizon lists, validation flags, and that the final-evaluation flag remains unopened for every selected record.
Only after this audit may the explicit \texttt{--unlock-test} gate be invoked.
The selected neural configurations are then retrained and evaluated with seeds 2023--2027.
Neural weights are fitted on training observations, with validation observations used for early stopping rather than gradient updates.
The complete neural matrix contains $8\times9\times5=360$ task--model--seed cells, each comprising six separately fitted horizon models.
Statistical references refuse to access the final period until all 360 neural cells are complete.

\subsection{Metrics and Prespecified Analysis}

For task $d$, horizon $H$, forecast origin $o$, and lead $h$, let $e_{d,o,h}=Y_{d,o,h}-\hat{Y}_{d,o,h}$.
Path MSE averages $e_{d,o,h}^2$ over every available origin and all leads $1,\ldots,H$; terminal MSE uses only lead $H$.
MAE and RMSE are defined analogously.
RMSSE divides MSE by the mean squared one-step difference of the inverse-transformed training target and then takes the square root \cite{m5}:
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{RMSSE}_{d,H}=
    \sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{MSE}_{d,H}}
    {(T_d-1)^{-1}\sum_{t=2}^{T_d}(Y_{d,t}-Y_{d,t-1})^2}}.
\end{equation}
Metrics remain task-specific; raw errors are never averaged across targets with different units.
Balanced RMSSE is the unweighted mean over the six horizons, equivalently the mean of the short ($1,5$), middle ($20,60$), and long ($120,252$) group means.

For each fitted model, skill relative to last-value is
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{Skill}_{d,H}=1-
    \frac{\operatorname{MSE}_{d,H}^{\mathrm{model}}}
         {\operatorname{MSE}_{d,H}^{\mathrm{LV}}}.
\end{equation}
Positive values indicate lower path MSE than persistence.
Neural metrics are summarized as mean and standard deviation over five seeds.
S and MS estimates are paired by panel, model, and horizon.

For confirmatory contrasts, origin-level squared losses are first averaged across the five neural seeds.
Each proposed variant (NS-Mamba-D, MS-Mamba-MD, and PMS-Mamba-PD) is paired with S-Mamba and with the non-Mamba model selected on validation for the same task.
A circular moving-block bootstrap resamples the paired origin-level loss difference 10,000 times.
The block length is $\min\{n,\max(H,\lceil n^{1/3}\rceil)\}$, and percentile 95\% intervals are reported.
Within each task, proposal, and comparator, two-sided bootstrap $p$-values across the six horizons receive Holm adjustment.

The prespecified secondary regime analysis assigns each origin using the standard deviation of target first differences over the preceding 60 observations, lagged by one observation.
Training-period 0.25 and 0.75 quantiles define low-, middle-, and high-volatility regimes.
Return forecasting is deferred until the primary level benchmark is complete and cannot replace an unfavorable level result.
