GLD was downloaded from Yahoo Finance with ticker \texttt{GLD} and \texttt{auto\_adjust=False}; the target is therefore the unadjusted close rather than an adjusted return series \cite{yahoo-gld}.
The local snapshot spans 2004-11-18 to 2026-04-30.
Its training, validation, and test targets end on 2019-11-18, 2022-01-10, and 2026-04-30, respectively.

The Exchange source is a repository-local long-format compilation identified as originating from FRED \cite{fred}.
The preparation script \path{scripts/prepare_exchange_uk.py} selects rows labelled \texttt{United Kingdom}, removes missing source values, sorts by date, and renames the value to \texttt{OT}.
The resulting series spans 1971-01-04 to 2026-05-15, with target boundaries at 2009-09-17, 2015-04-06, and 2026-05-15.
The raw retrieval record and exact FRED series identifier were not preserved; the quote direction and unit therefore cannot be independently verified from the repository and the target is treated as a quoted rate rather than assigned an economic unit.

Validation and test datasets include up to \texttt{seq\_len} earlier observations as input context, but their forecast targets remain within the nominal split.
The loader fits a \texttt{StandardScaler} on the training target only and applies it to all splits.
Predictions and targets are inverse-transformed before metrics are computed.

Weather was run exploratorily before a data-quality defect was identified: 50 training-target values encoded as \texttt{-9999} were neither replaced nor masked before scaling and training.
Its exclusion is therefore a post hoc protocol amendment made after exploratory outcomes existed, not a prespecified dataset filter.
No Weather value contributes to the reported comparison; a documented cleaning rule and complete rerun of every method are required before it can be used as evidence.

\subsection{Metrics, Origins, and Aggregation}

For a nominal forecast length $H$, each test example predicts all leads $h=1,\ldots,H$.
With $T_{\mathrm{test}}$ target observations, the sliding-window loader yields $O_H=T_{\mathrm{test}}-H+1$ origins and $N_H=O_HH$ scalar forecast errors.
The reported value for length $H$ pools every origin and all leads up to $H$; it is not an error calculated only at lead $H$.
Thus, origin counts range from 1,079 to 720 for GLD and from 2,777 to 2,418 for Exchange as $H$ increases from 1 to 360.

For predictions $\hat{Y}$ and targets $Y$, root mean squared error is
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{RMSE}
    =
    \sqrt{\frac{1}{N_H}\sum_{i=1}^{N_H}(Y_i-\hat{Y}_i)^2}.
\end{equation}
RMSSE normalizes forecast error by one-step changes in the inverse-transformed training target $X_{1:T}$ \cite{m5}:
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{RMSSE}
    =
    \sqrt{
        \frac{N_H^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_H}(Y_i-\hat{Y}_i)^2}
        {(T-1)^{-1}\sum_{t=2}^{T}(X_t-X_{t-1})^2}
    }.
\end{equation}
The denominator is fixed within a dataset but differs across datasets, so raw RMSSE is compared only within each dataset.

Short, middle, and long sets are $\{1,3,5,7\}$, $\{14,30,60,90\}$, and $\{120,180,270,360\}$.
If $\bar{R}_{g}$ denotes mean RMSSE within group $g$, the study-level aggregation is
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{BalancedRMSSE}
    = \frac{1}{3}\left(\bar{R}_{\mathrm{short}}+\bar{R}_{\mathrm{middle}}+\bar{R}_{\mathrm{long}}\right).
\end{equation}
Because every group contains four forecast lengths here, this quantity is also the arithmetic mean of the 12 RMSSE values.
It is a protocol-specific summary and does not create independent replications from the 12 overlapping tasks.

\subsection{Training and Hyperparameter Selection}

Each learned model--horizon configuration is trained separately with Adam, at most 10 epochs, early-stopping patience 3, and configured seed 2023.
Optuna uses a seeded TPE sampler and median pruning.
The neural baseline family contains Autoformer, DLinear, Informer, NLinear, Non-stationary Transformer, PatchTST, Reformer, TimesNet, Transformer, and iTransformer.
The Mamba family contains S-Mamba, NS-Mamba-D, MS-Mamba-MD, and PMS-Mamba-PD.
Last-value is deterministic and receives no tuning budget.

The neural search first evaluates a calibration horizon set.
Up to five top completed trials are replayed across the full validation horizon suite, and the candidate with the lowest validation balanced RMSSE is selected.
Only that selected configuration is evaluated by the final-sweep code on the test split.
This procedure yields 336 trained-model test cells, 168 per dataset; the deterministic calculation adds 24 last-value cells.

Search budgets are not identical.
GLD stores 20 trial records per baseline and Mamba model, but its refreshed neural-baseline search calibrates on all 12 horizons while the Mamba search calibrates on three.
Exchange stores 20 neural-baseline trial records and 24 Mamba trial records per model.
Search spaces are model-specific, and some archived studies contain interrupted or failed states.
Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-optuna-protocol} reports the exact records; comparisons are therefore model-specific tuned point estimates rather than equal-compute optimization results.

The split discipline above describes each final sweep, not the complete research cycle.
Repository development records show that earlier GLD test outcomes informed promotion of decomposition and patch-temporal variants before the reported benchmark was frozen.
Accordingly, the current GLD test period is development evidence and cannot support an untouched confirmatory claim.

\subsection{Result and Software Provenance}

The four trained-model result matrices follow \path{results/<dataset>_<phase>_optuna_best.csv}; selected configurations and trial histories are stored under \path{results/optuna/}.
The last-value rows are deterministically regenerated from the two target files by \path{manuscript/scripts/generate_benchmark_results.py} and written to \path{manuscript/data/last_value_baseline.csv}.
The same generator checks model--horizon coverage, completion state, split flags, metric finiteness, group aggregation, and agreement with selected-parameter metadata before writing the tables.

All 216 Exchange and GLD-Mamba trained rows match saved run-level \texttt{metrics.npy} files.
The 120 GLD neural-baseline rows are frozen in the authoritative CSV and a reproduction bundle containing parameters, checkpoints, predictions, and replay summaries.
The pinned software environment records Python 3.9.23, PyTorch 2.6.0 with CUDA 12.6 build, \texttt{mamba-ssm} 2.2.4, NumPy 1.23.5, pandas 2.3.1, and scikit-learn 1.6.1.
The original GPU model, runtime, memory use, and energy consumption were not recorded, so no computational-efficiency comparison is made.
