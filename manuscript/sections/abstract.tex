\begin{abstract}
Nonstationary time series forecasting is difficult because the data-generating process can change across time.
Changes in level, volatility, trend, and temporal regime can make a model trained on past windows unreliable for future horizons.
This manuscript studies nonstationarity-aware forecasting with Mamba-based sequence models, using daily GLD close-price prediction as the initial testbed.
The proposed model families extend an S-Mamba backbone with adaptive normalization, series decomposition, distribution-shift-aware fusion, and multi-scale Mamba branches.
The current manuscript reports only hyperparameter-searched result tracks across 12 horizons from 1 to 360 days.
Within the directly comparable optimized baseline sweep, iTransformer is the strongest overall model, while S-Mamba remains the strongest Mamba-style baseline before broader Mamba-family tuning.
After Mamba-specific hyperparameter optimization, MS-Mamba-MD becomes the strongest completed Mamba-family model.
On the promoted four-horizon summary over 120, 180, 270, and 360 days, it outperforms optimized NLinear and TimesNet.
The completed source-optimized imported-model sweep further shows that PatchTST is the strongest imported long-horizon baseline among the currently completed imported runs.
PatchTST remains the strongest overall long-horizon model in the current optimized evidence, so the manuscript treats MS-Mamba-MD as the promoted Mamba candidate rather than as a final state-of-the-art claim.
\end{abstract}
