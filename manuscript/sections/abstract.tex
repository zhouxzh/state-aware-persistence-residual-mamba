\begin{abstract}
Nonstationary time series forecasting is difficult because the data-generating process can change across time.
Changes in level, volatility, trend, and temporal regime can make a model trained on past windows unreliable for future horizons.
This manuscript studies nonstationarity-aware forecasting with Mamba-based sequence models, using daily GLD close-price prediction as the initial testbed.
The proposed model families extend an S-Mamba backbone with adaptive normalization, series decomposition, distribution-shift-aware fusion, and multi-scale Mamba branches.
The current manuscript reports only hyperparameter-searched result tracks across 12 horizons from 1 to 360 days.
Within the refreshed Optuna-tuned non-Mamba baseline sweep, RMSSE-based evaluation identifies TimesNet as the strongest overall model, iTransformer as a near-tied runner-up, NLinear as the short-horizon leader, PatchTST as the middle-horizon leader, and TimesNet/iTransformer as split long-horizon winners.
After Mamba-specific Optuna search, PMS-Mamba-PD becomes the strongest completed Mamba-family model.
On the promoted four-horizon summary over 120, 180, 270, and 360 days, PMS-Mamba-PD reaches RMSSE 27.25, RMSE 34.17, and MAE 23.72.
This is a 3.40\% RMSSE reduction relative to the strongest Optuna-tuned non-Mamba baseline on the same promoted horizon set.
Because the current evidence is limited to GLD and the manuscript is still a single-dataset draft, it treats PMS-Mamba-PD as the current strongest completed Mamba result rather than as a universal state-of-the-art claim.
\end{abstract}
