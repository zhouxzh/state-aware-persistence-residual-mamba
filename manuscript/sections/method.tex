\section{PMS-Mamba}

\subsection{Forecasting Task and Notation}

Let $\mathbf{X}_{t-L+1:t}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times C}$ be the observations available at forecast origin $t$, and let $\mathbf{Y}_{t+1:t+H}\in\mathbb{R}^{H}$ be the future path of one target series.
The target-only task uses $C=1$.
The paired multivariate-input task uses all $C>1$ panel variables while evaluating only the target stored in the final channel.
Thus, the multivariate task tests whether contemporaneously available histories improve one target forecast; it is not a claim of unrestricted multivariate forecasting or causal cross-market influence.
Separate models are fitted for horizons
\begin{equation}
    H\in\{1,5,20,60,120,252\}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Why Temporal Patches Are Needed}

Mamba is an input-selective state-space model with updates
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}_j=\bar{\mathbf{A}}_j\mathbf{h}_{j-1}+\bar{\mathbf{B}}_j\mathbf{e}_j,
    \qquad
    \mathbf{o}_j=\mathbf{C}_j\mathbf{h}_j,
\end{equation}
where the transition and readout parameters depend on the input token $\mathbf{e}_j$ \cite{mamba}.
The recurrence is only meaningful along the sequence presented to it.
S-Mamba uses an inverted embedding that maps each complete variable history to one token \cite{smamba}.
It therefore receives $C$ tokens rather than $L$ temporal tokens.

\begin{proposition}[Single-token limitation]
\label{prop:single-token}
For a target-only input ($C=1$), an inverted variable-token encoder presents exactly one token to its state-space block. Its output may depend on the complete input history through the embedding, but the state-space block has no transition between distinct temporal tokens.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:single-token} does not imply that S-Mamba is incapable of forecasting a univariate series; its embedding and projection remain learned functions of the input window.
It identifies a mismatch between a temporal state-space motivation and a one-token implementation.
PMS-Mamba resolves that mismatch by tokenizing each normalized time history into ordered temporal patches.

\subsection{Decomposition-Aware Multi-Scale Patch Encoder}

For every input window, PMS-Mamba first applies reversible per-window normalization.
For a batch item $b$ and channel $c$,
\begin{equation}
    \mu_{b,c}=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{t=1}^{L}X_{b,t,c},
    \qquad
    \sigma_{b,c}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{L}\sum_{t=1}^{L}(X_{b,t,c}-\mu_{b,c})^2+\epsilon},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    Z_{b,t,c}=\frac{X_{b,t,c}-\mu_{b,c}}{\sigma_{b,c}}.
\end{equation}
The final forecast is returned to the input window's location and scale.
This operation stabilizes the representation under local changes in level and magnitude; because $\epsilon>0$, it is not claimed to provide exact affine equivariance.

Let $\mathcal{M}_m$ denote the boundary-replicated moving average with window $m$ used in the implementation.
PMS-Mamba constructs three additive components,
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{T} &= \mathcal{M}_m(\mathbf{Z}), \\
    \mathbf{D} &= \mathbf{Z}-\mathbf{T}, \\
    \mathbf{S} &= \mathcal{M}_m(\mathbf{D}), \\
    \mathbf{R} &= \mathbf{D}-\mathbf{S}.
\end{align}
The symbols $\mathbf{T}$, $\mathbf{S}$, and $\mathbf{R}$ denote a smoothed component, a smoothed residual, and a residual component, respectively.
They are architectural representations, not identified trend, seasonal, or structural financial processes.

For each scale $s\in\mathcal{S}=\{1,2,4\}$, average pooling constructs
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{V}^{(s)}=\mathcal{P}_s\!\left(\operatorname{concat}_{C}[\mathbf{T},\mathbf{S},\mathbf{R}]\right).
\end{equation}
Each channel of $\mathbf{V}^{(s)}$ is divided into ordered, right-padded patches of length $p$ and stride $r$.
The patch embedding is followed by a bidirectional Mamba encoder and a flattened projection head.
If $g_s$ denotes this scale-specific mapping, its output is regrouped into the three components and summed within each original variable:
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}^{(s)}_c=
    \sum_{k\in\{T,S,R\}}g_{s,k,c}(\mathbf{V}^{(s)}),
    \qquad
    \widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}=
    \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}^{(s)}.
\end{equation}
The frozen PMS-Mamba-PD configuration uses this fixed mean scale fusion rather than learned or shift-gated fusion.

\subsection{Representation Properties}

The following propositions concern the fixed representation and aggregation operations in PMS-Mamba, not the unknown data-generating process or the nonlinear learned branches.

\begin{proposition}[Additive component preservation]
\label{prop:additive}
For every normalized input window $\mathbf{Z}$, the implemented decomposition satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{Z}=\mathbf{T}+\mathbf{S}+\mathbf{R}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Stable pooling and bounded mean fusion]
\label{prop:stable-fusion}
For any two component tensors $\mathbf{U}$ and $\mathbf{V}$, every average-pooling operator used by PMS-Mamba satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \left\|\mathcal{P}_s(\mathbf{U})-\mathcal{P}_s(\mathbf{V})\right\|_{\infty}
    \leq \left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{\infty}.
\end{equation}
For fixed scale-specific forecasts, each coordinate of fixed mean fusion lies between the minimum and maximum of that coordinate over the scales.
\end{proposition}

Proofs appear in Appendix~\ref{app:theory}.
The first property prevents information loss before learned component branches; the second shows that pooling and fixed fusion do not amplify a coordinatewise perturbation by themselves.
Neither property establishes forecast accuracy, and both require empirical evaluation of the learned maps $g_s$.

\subsection{Ablation Logic and Comparators}

The implemented Mamba configurations isolate progressively richer representations: S-Mamba uses one inverted variable-token path; NS-Mamba-D adds the additive decomposition; MS-Mamba-MD adds multi-scale component tokens; and PMS-Mamba-PD replaces the variable-token encoder with temporal patch encoders.
These comparisons do not constitute a complete factorial ablation because decomposition, scale construction, and tokenization change jointly in some transitions.
The recomputed study will therefore add matched patch-only, decomposition-only, multi-scale-only, and full PMS-Mamba variants with parameter counts reported for every model.

The external learned comparators are DLinear, NLinear, PatchTST, TimesNet, and iTransformer \cite{dlinear,patchtst,timesnet,itransformer}.
Last-value, random-walk-with-drift, and ARIMA provide univariate references; VAR on first differences provides a multivariate reference.
ARIMA orders $(p,d,q)$ are selected on training data by AIC over $d\in\{0,1\}$ and $p,q\in\{0,1,2,3\}$, excluding ARIMA$(0,0,0)$.
For multivariate tasks, VAR lags from 1 through 10 are selected by training BIC and forecasts are accumulated from differences to levels.
The statistical reference coefficients are estimated once from all pre-test training and validation observations and their states are updated sequentially with observed values; neural weights use training observations while validation controls early stopping.

\subsection{Training Objective}

All learned models minimize target-channel mean squared error on the scaled representation,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{BH}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{h=1}^{H}
    \left(\hat{Y}_{b,h}-Y_{b,h}\right)^2.
\end{equation}
Forecasts and targets are transformed back to the target's original scale before evaluation.
