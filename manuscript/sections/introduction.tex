\section{Introduction}

Financial forecasting is frequently evaluated on price or rate levels that combine strong persistence with changes in location, scale, volatility, and dependence across markets.
These properties complicate both modeling and evaluation.
A flexible model can appear accurate by reproducing persistence, while a random chronological split can expose it to a future period unlike the one used for fitting.
Accordingly, evidence for a forecasting architecture requires explicit reference forecasts, fixed temporal boundaries, selection separated from final evaluation, and uncertainty estimates that respect dependent forecast origins \cite{m4,m5,finance-dl-survey}.

Modern long-horizon forecasters use linear decompositions, temporal patches, two-dimensional temporal representations, or inverted variable tokens \cite{dlinear,patchtst,timesnet,itransformer}.
Mamba provides an input-selective state-space operator with sequence-length scaling that is linear in the recurrent scan \cite{mamba}.
Forecasting adaptations differ in whether the scan acts across variables or temporal tokens and whether decomposition or multiple temporal resolutions are introduced \cite{smamba,bimamba,mambats,timemachine,msmamba,karma,dmamba}.
These architectural differences are plausible responses to changing temporal structure, but plausibility alone does not show that they improve out-of-period financial forecasts.

This study asks three bounded questions.
First, do the tested Mamba variants improve level forecasts relative to persistence, drift, statistical models, and tuned non-Mamba neural baselines?
Second, are any differences stable across foreign-exchange, yield-curve, cross-asset, and cryptoasset panels and across forecast horizons from one observation to approximately one trading year?
Third, does adding contemporaneously available panel histories improve single-target forecasts relative to the paired univariate-input task?
The study predicts statistical levels rather than returns and does not evaluate trading profitability.

To answer these questions without reusing the development evidence, we froze version 1 of the financial confirmatory protocol before opening its final period.
Four panels generate eight paired tasks: S mode uses only the target history, whereas MS mode supplies the complete panel history but evaluates only the named target.
Nine learned models share a 12-trial selection budget per task and are evaluated at six horizons using five final seeds.
The final period, 2023-01-01 through 2026-06-30, is protected by an explicit software gate until all validation selections and their hashes pass audit.
Legacy GLD and Exchange experiments informed development and are excluded from confirmatory claims; non-financial application benchmarks are outside the frozen scope.

The contributions of the study design are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a checksum-bound, fixed-calendar benchmark spanning four financial targets, paired S/MS tasks, six horizons, and five final seeds;
    \item an implementation-grounded comparison of inverted, decomposition, multi-scale, and patch-temporal Mamba configurations against neural and statistical references;
    \item a prespecified analysis of path and terminal errors, improvement relative to last-value, paired S/MS differences, volatility regimes, and dependence-aware bootstrap contrasts; and
    \item fail-closed result generation that withholds final tables until every required model, seed, task, horizon, and provenance field is present.
\end{itemize}

This version is an interim, protocol-aligned manuscript.
It reports only training/validation data characterization; performance results and conclusions remain deliberately unwritten until the locked matrix is complete.
