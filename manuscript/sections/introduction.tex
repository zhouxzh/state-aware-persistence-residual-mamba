\section{Introduction}

Long-horizon time series forecasting is important in applications where decisions must be made well before the target is observed, including energy systems, transportation, operations planning, and financial markets \cite{informer,autoformer,tft,tsf-survey}.
In this setting, forecasting performance depends not only on short-term pattern extraction but also on the ability to preserve useful temporal information across long contexts while remaining stable under changing data distributions.
These requirements are difficult to satisfy simultaneously, which is why long-horizon forecasting remains an active research problem across both statistical and deep-learning paradigms \cite{nbeats,nhits,dlinear,patchtst,timesnet}.

The difficulty is amplified when the underlying series is nonstationary.
Real-world financial series can undergo level shifts, volatility changes, trend reversals, and regime-dependent behavior driven by macroeconomic events, policy changes, and evolving market expectations \cite{finance-dl-survey}.
Under such conditions, a model trained on historical windows may face a materially different prediction problem at test time.
This issue is especially relevant for gold-related exchange-traded products such as GLD, whose price dynamics can reflect inflation expectations, risk-off behavior, U.S. dollar movements, and global uncertainty.
For this reason, long-horizon GLD forecasting provides a useful testbed for studying whether modern sequence models can remain effective when temporal dependence and distribution shift appear together.

Recent forecasting research has introduced a broad set of architectures for long-context modeling, including efficient Transformers, decomposition-based models, patch-based encoders, and inverted variable-token representations \cite{informer,autoformer,fedformer,itransformer,patchtst}.
At the same time, recent studies have shown that explicitly addressing distribution shift and nonstationarity can improve robustness in forecasting pipelines \cite{revin,nonstationary-transformer}.
Mamba adds another attractive direction because it replaces quadratic attention with selective state-space sequence modeling while retaining strong expressive capacity and linear-time scaling \cite{mamba}.
This makes Mamba a promising backbone for long-horizon forecasting, and several recent papers have adapted it to time-series settings \cite{smamba,bimamba,mambats,timemachine,tsmamba}.
However, efficient sequence modeling alone does not resolve the core difficulty of nonstationary forecasting: level, scale, trend, and residual behavior may still be entangled in a single hidden representation even when the backbone itself is computationally efficient.

This manuscript studies that gap through a nonstationarity-aware Mamba forecasting framework for daily GLD close-price prediction across horizons from 1 to 360 days.
The proposed NS-Mamba family preserves an S-Mamba-style backbone while adding adaptive normalization, decomposition, and shift-aware fusion modules designed to expose nonstationary structure before and around sequence modeling.
To make comparisons interpretable while still allowing model-specific tuning, the manuscript evaluates an Optuna-tuned non-Mamba baseline sweep alongside a separate Mamba-family Optuna search.
This produces a current view of how Transformer-style, decomposition-based, inverted, and Mamba-based models behave after lookback and architecture tuning rather than under one locked default setting.

The current evidence leads to a deliberately narrow conclusion.
In the refreshed Optuna-tuned non-Mamba sweep, RMSSE-based evaluation identifies TimesNet as the strongest overall baseline and iTransformer as a near-tied runner-up.
NLinear leads the short horizons, PatchTST leads the middle horizons, and TimesNet/iTransformer split the long-horizon wins.
Within the Mamba family, the current RMSSE/RMSE/MAE Optuna search identifies PMS-Mamba-PD as the strongest completed Mamba variant on both the shared 120/180/360 subset and the promoted 120/180/270/360 long-horizon summary.
Because the current evidence is limited to GLD and this is still a draft, the paper avoids a universal state-of-the-art claim.

The main contributions of this manuscript are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item it defines a GLD forecasting benchmark spanning 12 prediction horizons from 1 to 360 days, with Optuna-tuned non-Mamba and Mamba-family search tracks;
    \item it introduces an NS-Mamba architecture family that combines an S-Mamba backbone with adaptive normalization, decomposition, and shift-aware fusion for nonstationary forecasting;
    \item it reports Optuna-tuned non-Mamba baseline results and Mamba-family search results under RMSSE-based evaluation, with RMSE and MAE retained for price-scale interpretation;
    \item it identifies PMS-Mamba-PD as the strongest completed Mamba-family configuration in the current hyperparameter search and compares it against the strongest Optuna-tuned non-Mamba RMSSE references.
\end{itemize}
