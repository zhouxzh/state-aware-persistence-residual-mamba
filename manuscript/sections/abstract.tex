\begin{abstract}
Selective state-space models are increasingly used for time-series forecasting, but their performance relative to tuned attention, linear, decomposition, patch-based, and persistence methods remains protocol- and dataset-dependent.
This descriptive study evaluates four Mamba variants, ten non-Mamba neural baselines, and a deterministic last-value forecast on two univariate financial level series: the GLD unadjusted close and a United Kingdom exchange-rate series.
The neural comparison comprises 336 model--horizon test cells, supplemented by 24 last-value cells, across 12 forecast lengths and chronological 70/10/20 splits.
Within the completed neural sweeps, hyperparameters are selected on validation data; the reported test scores are nevertheless treated as development-period evidence because earlier GLD test outcomes informed architecture promotion.
On GLD, PMS-Mamba-PD records balanced RMSSE 14.74, below TimesNet at 15.66 and last-value at 17.07.
On Exchange, last-value is lowest at 6.26, followed by S-Mamba at 6.29 and PatchTST at 6.37; thus, no learned model improves on persistence for that series under the current protocol.
These are single-seed point estimates from overlapping forecast windows, with unequal search budgets and no inferential intervals.
The results support conditional competitiveness of the tested Mamba variants on GLD, not general superiority or a confirmatory financial-forecasting claim.
\end{abstract}
