\section{Results}

\subsection{Dataset-Level Point Estimates}

\input{sections/results_benchmark_summary}

Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-family-summary} changes the interpretation of the neural-only comparison by retaining persistence.
On GLD, PMS-Mamba-PD records balanced RMSSE 14.74, compared with 15.66 for TimesNet and 17.07 for last-value.
These point estimates correspond to a 5.89\% reduction relative to TimesNet and a 13.66\% reduction relative to last-value.
They show that the selected PMS-Mamba-PD run is competitive on this GLD test period, but they do not quantify sampling or training uncertainty.

On Exchange, last-value records the lowest balanced RMSSE, 6.26.
S-Mamba is the lowest learned model at 6.29, approximately 0.58\% higher than last-value, while PatchTST records 6.37.
Consequently, the earlier neural-only statement that S-Mamba improves on the strongest baseline does not survive inclusion of the deterministic reference.

Figure~\ref{fig:benchmark-family-comparison} avoids a per-horizon family oracle: every learned curve represents one model across the complete length set.
RMSSE increases with nominal forecast length for all displayed methods because each length pools progressively more distant leads.
The GLD curves separate more visibly at intermediate and long lengths, whereas the Exchange curves remain close and the persistence curve is lowest in the aggregate.

\subsection{Model-Level Comparison}

\input{sections/results_model_ranking}

Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-model-ranking} reports every evaluated method without pooling ranks across the two targets.
Among Mamba variants, PMS-Mamba-PD is lowest on GLD, while S-Mamba is lowest on Exchange; NS-Mamba-D is close to the selected Mamba model on both series.
No Mamba configuration is lowest on both datasets once last-value is included.
The observed ordering therefore supports a dataset-specific description, not a universal architectural ranking.
