There are several plausible reasons why Autoformer, Transformer, Informer, and Reformer are much weaker than S\_Mamba and iTransformer on this GLD task.
First, the two strongest models use an inverted embedding scheme that maps the full historical window into variable tokens before forecasting, while the four weaker baselines use the standard time-step sequence embedding with encoder-decoder style generation.
For a single-target task with a long lookback window, the inverted formulation appears to be better aligned with direct horizon projection than the standard decoder-based setup.
Second, S\_Mamba and iTransformer both apply per-window mean and variance normalization with final de-normalization, which is likely important for GLD because the price level and volatility change substantially across time.
Transformer, Informer, and Reformer in this repository do not apply the same reversible normalization path, and Autoformer applies decomposition but not the same explicit nonstationary normalization.
Third, the baseline comparison deliberately uses one shared setting selected around S\_Mamba, so the result should be interpreted as a controlled comparison under one configuration rather than as a per-model hyperparameter optimum.
Even with that caveat, the size and consistency of the gap suggest that architecture-data mismatch, not only tuning, is a major factor.
The completed source-optimized imported sweep reinforces this point from the opposite direction: once model-specific hyperparameters are allowed, PatchTST, NLinear, and TimesNet all become much stronger than their locked-setting counterparts, which means GLD is unusually sensitive to lookback and model-scale choices.
The same conclusion holds for the Mamba family: after Mamba-specific hyperparameter optimization, the best MS-Mamba variant moves ahead of optimized NLinear and TimesNet on the completed long-horizon diagnostic average, although it still trails optimized PatchTST.
