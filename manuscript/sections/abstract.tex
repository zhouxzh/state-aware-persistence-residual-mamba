\begin{abstract}
Financial level series combine persistent local levels with changes in location, scale, volatility, and cross-series dependence. We introduce PMS-Mamba, a decomposition-aware multi-scale patch Mamba model for this setting. The model first reversibly normalizes each input window, decomposes it into moving-average trend, smoothed-residual, and residual components, constructs several mean-pooled temporal resolutions, and applies bidirectional Mamba blocks to ordered temporal patches at each resolution. This design differs from inverted variable-token Mamba encoders: for a univariate task, their state-space scan receives a single variable token and cannot traverse temporal tokens. We establish that the implemented decomposition is additive, that its pooling stage is non-expansive in the maximum norm, and that fixed mean fusion remains coordinatewise bounded by the scale-specific forecasts. Selection-safe analysis of four daily financial panels---foreign exchange, U.S. Treasury yields, cross-asset prices, and cryptoassets---finds level nonstationarity under the prespecified ADF/KPSS diagnostic pair, validation mean shifts from 0.51 to 7.75 training standard deviations in magnitude, and first-difference volatility ratios from 0.82 to 4.52. These observations motivate, but do not prove, the model's inductive biases. The final 2023--2026 out-of-sample evaluation is not yet reported; therefore, this version makes no claim of forecast superiority.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} financial forecasting; nonstationary time series; Mamba; state-space models; multi-scale analysis
