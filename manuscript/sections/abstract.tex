\begin{abstract}
Nonstationary time series forecasting is difficult because the data-generating process can change across time.
Changes in level, volatility, trend, and temporal regime can make a model trained on past windows unreliable for future horizons.
This manuscript studies nonstationarity-aware forecasting with Mamba-based sequence models, using daily GLD close-price prediction as the initial testbed.
The proposed model families extend an S\_Mamba backbone with adaptive normalization, series decomposition, distribution-shift-aware fusion, and multi-scale Mamba branches.
Before evaluating the proposed architecture, we define a controlled baseline protocol across 12 horizons from 1 to 360 days and lock a shared configuration through S\_Mamba calibration.
The completed GLD baseline results show that S\_Mamba is the strongest model from 1 to 90 days on RMSE, while iTransformer is strongest from 120 to 360 days.
The first NS-Mamba diagnostic ablation shows that the decomposition-only variant improves long-horizon RMSE over both S\_Mamba and iTransformer.
After Mamba-specific hyperparameter optimization, MS-Mamba-MD becomes the strongest Mamba-family model and outperforms optimized NLinear and TimesNet on the completed long-horizon diagnostic average.
The completed source-optimized imported-model sweep further shows that PatchTST is the strongest imported baseline overall when its own hyperparameters are tuned rather than forced into the shared setting.
PatchTST remains the strongest overall long-horizon model in the current evidence, so the manuscript treats MS-Mamba-MD as the promoted Mamba candidate rather than as a final state-of-the-art claim.
\end{abstract}
